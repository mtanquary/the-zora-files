# Finding Zora

Zora means "dawn" in Serbian, Croatian, Czech, Slovak, Slovenian, Bulgarian and Macedonian. This system is the foundation for everything Tank makes under the name Finding Zora: videos, posts, the website, and the gear he wears and hands out. The goal is a recognizable flavor, not one repeated picture. The Z mark is the only constant; everything else varies by piece while staying unmistakably Zora.

## The names and what each one does

- **Finding Zora** is the forward name and the brand: the show, the posts, and the game it grew from ("Finding Zora, the Game"). Every trip is a hunt for the sunrise and the story along the way.
- **ZORA** plus the Z is the short form for gear, where one word and a symbol read better than two words.
- **Finders** is the working name for the community: the people who go looking. Saying "you're a Finder now" goes with handing someone a coin. It stays a spoken concept, not printed on gear, until it proves it has legs.
- **The Zora Files** is the archive and the website (thezorafiles.com), the record of every hunt. Keep #thezorafiles and add #findingzora.
- **"Zora found."** is the on-camera sign-off the moment the sun clears the horizon.

## Purpose

Find the story behind what you're looking at: the 1916 bridge, the toad waiting out drought in a rock crack, the mountain that's secretly a collapsed supervolcano. The creed for the back of the coin is **"Up before the sun. Find the story."** A second line for reveals on camera: "There's always more to it." Treat the purpose as a 90-day trial before it goes on printed gear.

## The flavor: three ingredients

Every piece uses the Z plus at least two of these. Using all three on one item is usually too much.

1. **First light.** The palette is the sky in the 20 minutes before sunrise: `blue-hour`, `dawn-rose`, `first-light`, with `bone` and `rhyolite` as ground and stone. `lichen` is the surprise.
2. **Field issue.** Gear that looks issued rather than marketed: the stencil display face, survey-benchmark circles (the brass disks set on summits), squared patches, wide-tracked rim text. It nods to the veteran and challenge-coin tradition without camouflage or flags.
3. **The record.** Real field data set in the `stamp` style: coordinates, date, first-light time, Eos score. Because the data changes with every place, each tee, pin or print can be unique while looking like part of one set.

## The Z mark

The Z is a switchback trail. It reads as a true Z: it starts at the trailhead (bottom right), runs left to the first bend, climbs the diagonal to the upper right, and the top line becomes the horizon, with the sun rising off its left end where the trail finishes. It carries the tagline: the beauty is the journey you took to capture it.

It is drawn with quiet trail cues so it reads as a path rather than a letter: rounded hairpin turns at both bends, a slight drift on each leg with softly worn edges, a trail that narrows from the trailhead toward the horizon, two stones set in the tread near the trailhead, and a three-stone cairn standing beside the first bend.

- The sun is always `first-light` when color is available. The trail is `blue-hour` on light grounds and `bone` on dark ones. One-color jobs (embroidery on a budget, engraving, single-ink prints) use `zora-z-mono`.
- Clear space on every side equals the sun's height. Minimum size: 12 mm in print or embroidery, 24 px on screen. Below about 48 px the stones fade out and the cairn becomes a small bump; that is expected, and the hairpins carry the idea.
- Never mirror, rotate or outline it, add a gradient, fill the counters, swap the sun for an emoji or a photo, straighten the trail, or add more stones or cairns.
- The lockups use a heavier, narrower Z as the first letter of ZORA, with the same hairpins, drift, taper and stones but no cairn, so nothing crowds the O. "ora" stands close beside the Z, its top aligned with the Z's top and its baseline riding just above the Z's bottom line, so the trailhead end tucks under the O. `finding-zora-lockup` (FINDING tracked to the width of ZORA) is the primary brand lockup for thumbnails, the website header, title cards and tee backs. `zora-lockup` is the short form where space is tight. Use the Z alone everywhere else, like a swoosh.

## Color

Rough balance on any piece: 60% ground (`bone`, `blue-hour`, `char` or `rhyolite`), 30% ink, 10% `first-light`. `lichen` and `dawn-rose` appear at most once per piece. Text rules: `ink` on `ground` in both themes; `first-light` text only on `blue-hour` or darker (7.7:1); never `first-light` or `lichen` text on `bone` (1.7:1 and 1.5:1).

## Type

- `display` (Big Shoulders Stencil Display, weight 800) for the wordmark, headlines and rim text. All caps is fine here.
- `body` (Overpass) for captions and copy. Overpass descends from the Highway Gothic road-sign letters, which suits a road-trip brand.
- `mono` (Overpass Mono) only for field-stamp data.

## Voice

Deadpan and fact-forward, short enough to read in one glance, never textbook. Hooks bury the reveal until the last beat. Examples: "Why is this town called Why." "This mountain is a supervolcano." "Zora found."

## Gear

- **Hat.** Unstructured six-panel or rope cap in `blue-hour` or `rhyolite`. Front: the Z alone, embroidered with a `bone` stroke and `first-light` sun, no words. Side: a small coordinate line in tonal thread for home sunrise. Strap or back: "Zora found." This is the daily-wear piece, so it stays quiet.
- **Tee.** Front left chest: small Z. Back: a field stamp for one real place, large. Each run is a numbered "field issue" (No. 001 Kelvin-Riverside Bridge, No. 002 Chiricahua) so the shirts collect like patches. Colorways: `bone`, `char`, `rhyolite`.
- **Pin.** Hard enamel, gold plating standing in for `first-light`. The everyday pin is the Z. Place pins pair the Z with the place name and stamp number, so the set grows trip by trip.
- **Coin.** Front: the survey disk with FINDING ZORA on the top rim and UP BEFORE THE SUN on the bottom. Back: the creed and an engraved serial "No. ___" for the person and place it was handed out. Antique brass. No website URL, so it implies Zora rather than advertising it.
- **Patch.** Square or round, merrowed edge, hook backing. `blue-hour` twill, `bone` and `first-light` thread, FINDING ZORA in the `patch` style.

## Do not

Put the URL on gear meant to be handed out, use palm-tree or generic sunset silhouettes, run more than one gradient (photography is the only place a sunset gradient belongs), mix more than five brand colors on one piece, or set long text in the stencil face.
